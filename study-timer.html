Why a study timer helps more than a to-do list

A list tells you what needs doing. A student focus timer tells you when to begin. When revision feels endless, a 10- or 25-minute session makes the first page, first problem, or first paragraph small enough to start.

Keep the current assignment visible as a task. If your mind jumps to other work, dump it into notes and continue until the timer ends.

Suggested study-session lengths

  • 10 minutes — opening notes, rereading a heading, or starting a past paper.
  • 25 minutes — a standard revision block for most school or university work.
  • 40–50 minutes — deeper reading, coding practice, or writing when you already started.

Use the energy check in StartTiny if you are tired or overwhelmed. A shorter study timer is better than skipping the session.

A simple study routine

  1. Add one assignment or topic as a task. Add a deadline if it has one.
  2. Choose a study timer length you can actually finish.
  3. Start the timer, then open the materials.
  4. After the session, mark the task done or leave it for the next block.

This works for homework, group projects, language practice, exam revision, and independent reading. Adults can use the same study timer for certifications, professional exams, or learning a new skill.
